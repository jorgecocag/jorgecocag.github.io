Smart Dog Collar Chip

Advanced tracking and monitoring system for pets

Project Description

This electronic design project called Smart Dog Collar Chip is a compact device designed to monitor and track the dog wearing it. It integrates multiple sensors, a durable built-in battery, and Bluetooth communication modules to ensure the safety and well-being of your canine companion. The goal of this project is to monitor the health, physical activity, and treatment of dogs, with a special focus on guide dogs for the visually impaired.

Key Features

  • GNSS/GPS module footprint with RF keep-out routing.
  • Temperature sensor integrated via I²C.
  • 3-axis IMU (accelerometer & gyroscope) for motion sensing.
  • Dual MEMS microphones for audio-event capture.
  • PMIC-based power regulation for all system rails.
  • Battery input connector for Li-ion collar integration.
  • STM32 MCU with BLE antenna and matching network.
  • Separated analog / digital / RF layout zones.

Technical Specifications

  • Microcontroller: STM32WB05xZ with onboard BLE support; direct PCB-printed Bluetooth antenna.
  • Power Management: NEH7100BU PMIC; supports Li-ion battery input and USB-C charging interface.
  • Sensors: BME280 (temperature, pressure, humidity) and ICM-20948 (3-axis IMU); communicate via I²C.
  • GPS Module: MAX-8; communicates via UART.
  • Audio: Dual ICS43434 MEMS microphones; interface via I²S.
  • PCB Layout Highlights: Compact wearable-oriented form factor; analog, digital and RF separation; controlled trace routing for RF and I²S signals.

Project Roadmap

The Smart Dog Collar Chip PCB design followed a structured workflow focused on component selection, layout, and verification.

Phase 1: Concept & Feasibility

  • Assess PCB design possibilities and constraints
  • Define project scope and functional blocks
  • Evaluate potential components for MCU, sensors, PMIC, and connectivity

Phase 2: Component Selection & Footprints

  • Finalize selection of MCU, PMIC, sensors, GPS, and audio components
  • Create or verify footprints for all components in Altium
  • Ensure mechanical and electrical compatibility with intended board size

Phase 3: Placement & Schematic Design

  • Complete schematic capture for all components and interfaces
  • Optimize component placement for signal integrity, RF, and analog/digital separation
  • Consider power routing and decoupling strategies

Phase 4: PCB Routing & Layout

  • Route signal, power, and RF traces with controlled impedance where needed
  • Design BLE antenna directly on PCB and integrate GPS traces
  • Verify clearances, via placements, and board outline for wearable form factor

Phase 5: Verification & Review

  • Perform DRC (Design Rule Check) and ERC (Electrical Rule Check)
  • Inspect footprints, placement, and routing for manufacturability
  • Finalize PCB design for prototype fabrication

Technical Challenges & Solutions

Key PCB design challenges encountered and how they were addressed.

BLE Antenna Integration

Challenge: Designing a PCB-printed Bluetooth antenna without interference from digital and analog sections.

Solution: Separated RF, digital, and analog zones; implemented dedicated ground planes and controlled impedance routing for the antenna.

High-Density Placement

Challenge: Integrating MCU, PMIC, sensors, and connectors in a compact wearable-sized PCB.

Solution: Optimized component placement by functional blocks, used small-footprint components, and iteratively refined layout in KiCad.

Power Management

Challenge: Ensuring stable power rails for MCU and sensors with PMIC and battery input.

Solution: Used NEH7100BU PMIC with proper decoupling, separated power and signal traces, and placed capacitors close to ICs.

Mixed-Signal Routing

Challenge: Routing digital (I²C, I²S, UART) and analog/RF signals without interference.

Solution: Used short trace routing, differential lines for I²S, analog/digital separation, and controlled impedance where required.

Sensor Integration

Challenge: Connecting BME280 and ICM-20948 sensors reliably and maintaining easy access for debugging.

Solution: Placed sensors in functional blocks, isolated I²C lines from high-speed signals, and added test points for debugging.

Mechanical Constraints

Challenge: Designing the PCB to fit inside a dog-collar form factor with connectors and battery clearance.

Solution: Defined outline early, positioned connectors at edges, and verified clearances for battery and USB-C input.
